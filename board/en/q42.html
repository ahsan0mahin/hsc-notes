Question and stimulus

From a car moving east at 40 km/h a driver sees a truck moving north at twice that speed. Take east as positive x and north as positive y.

  1. (a)

    What is a postulate?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Compare the units and physical meanings of work and torque.

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    In what direction is the truck actually moving?

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Find a vector perpendicular to the plane of the two true velocities.

    4 marks

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is a postulate?

Hint Try it first

Distinguish an assumption from a deduction.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is a starting proposition accepted without proof within a theory.

Part b · 2 marks

Compare the units and physical meanings of work and torque.

Hint Try it first

Compare a dot product with a cross product.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Both have dimensions force times length. Work is a scalar in joules; torque is an axial vector in newton metres.

Part c · 3 marks

In what direction is the truck actually moving?

Hint Try it first

The truck’s apparent velocity is measured relative to the driver. Add back the driver’s ground velocity.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

v⃗T=v⃗TC+v⃗C=80j^+40i^\vec v_T=\vec v_{TC}+\vec v_C=80\hat j+40\hat i

Add the car velocity to the relative truck velocity: (40,80) km/h. The direction is 63.43° north of east.

Part d · 4 marks

Find a vector perpendicular to the plane of the two true velocities.

Hint Try it first

Apply a cross product to the ground velocities, not to the speed magnitudes.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

v⃗C×v⃗T=3200k^  (km/h)2\vec v_C\times\vec v_T=3200\hat k\;(\mathrm{km/h})^2

The car and truck velocities are in the xy-plane. Their cross product is (0,0,3200); the positive z unit vector is normal to the plane.

Source notes and corrections

The cross product of two velocities has squared-speed units. A unit normal gives a direction and is dimensionless.
