Question and stimulus

P, Q and R have magnitudes 3, 4 and 5 respectively, and P+Q+R=0.

  1. (a)

    State the right-hand screw rule.

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Is magnitude plus direction sufficient to make a quantity a vector?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find Q·R.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Can the magnitude of R×(P×Q) be determined? Show how.

    4 marks

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

State the right-hand screw rule.

Hint Try it first

Imagine turning a right-handed screw.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Turn it from the first vector towards the second through the smaller angle. Its advance gives the direction of their cross product.

Part b · 2 marks

Is magnitude plus direction sufficient to make a quantity a vector?

Hint Try it first

Consider how two such quantities combine.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

No. It must also transform and add according to the vector rules; finite rotations are a familiar counterexample.

Part c · 3 marks

Find Q·R.

Hint Try it first

Rearrange the closed-vector equation to isolate P, then square using the dot product.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

2Q⃗⋅R⃗=P2−Q2−R22\vec Q\cdot\vec R=P^2-Q^2-R^2

Squaring P=−(Q+R) gives 9=16+25+2Q·R. Hence Q·R=−16.

Part d · 4 marks

Can the magnitude of R×(P×Q) be determined? Show how.

Hint Try it first

Use the given side lengths to test the angle between P and Q. Where does R lie relative to P×Q?

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∣R⃗×(P⃗×Q⃗)∣=5(3)(4)=60|\vec R\times(\vec P\times\vec Q)|=5(3)(4)=60

Since 3²+4²=5², P and Q are perpendicular; their cross product has length 12. R lies in their plane, so it is perpendicular to P×Q. The required magnitude is 5×12=60.

Source notes and corrections

Part (d) asks for magnitude, not components. The triple-product identity would give the vector −16P+9Q.
