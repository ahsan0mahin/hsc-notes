Question and stimulus

Kuber sets out along OA at 12 km/h across a 10 km wide river with a 4 km/h current. After 10 min a radio warning gives him 30 min to reach safety. He raises the sail, adding 40% of a 20 km/h wind along OA. Wind effects were negligible before that.

    Define curl.

  2. (b)

    Why is a position vector localized?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Without the sail at what angle would he reach the far bank?

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Can he reach safety by the deadline?

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

OA crosses the 10 km width. The safe landing is marked downstream on the opposite bank. Raising the sail increases the across-bank component in the stated model.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define curl.

Hint Try it first

Imagine a tiny paddle wheel in the field.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Curl, ∇×V⃗\nabla\times\vec V, measures local circulation per unit area. Its direction is the local axis of rotation given by the right-hand rule.

Part b · 2 marks

Why is a position vector localized?

Hint Try it first

Change the tail without changing the arrow.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It begins at a specified origin. Moving its tail would no longer locate the same point relative to that origin.

Part c · 3 marks

Without the sail at what angle would he reach the far bank?

Hint Try it first

Before raising the sail, combine the current with the perpendicular rowing speed.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

tan⁡θ=12/4\tan\theta=12/4

With across and downstream components 12 and 4, the direction is arctan(12/4)=71.57° to the current.

Part d · 4 marks

Can he reach safety by the deadline?

Hint Try it first

Split the trip at the radio warning. Apply the wind contribution only to the remaining width.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

tafter=10−12(10/60)12+0.4(20) ht_{\rm after}=\frac{10-12(10/60)}{12+0.4(20)}\,\mathrm h

He covers 2 km across in the first 10 min. The remaining 8 km at 12+0.4×20=20 km/h takes 24 min, less than the remaining 30 min. This establishes the time to reach the bank; the exact safe landing must also match the downstream drift.

Source notes and corrections

The timing shows that the boat can reach the far bank. Reaching any particular marked safe landing also requires its position to agree with the downstream drift.
