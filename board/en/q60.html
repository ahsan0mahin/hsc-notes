Question and stimulus

A car travels at 4.62 m/s towards v; the wind blows in the opposite direction u. Rain falls vertically downwards towards M at 8 m/s before allowing for wind.

  1. (a)

    What is a polar vector?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Why does a rotating ceiling fan move air downward?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Explain with a diagram why rain hits the front window but not the rear in this setup.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Will holding an umbrella at 40° from vertical protect a person on the roof?

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

The car moves towards v, the wind towards u and the rain downwards towards m. Resolve velocities in the car’s frame before judging the incident angle.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is a polar vector?

Hint Try it first

Consider inversion of spatial coordinates.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

A polar vector transforms like displacement, reversing under inversion. Force and velocity are examples.

Part b · 2 marks

Why does a rotating ceiling fan move air downward?

Hint Try it first

Distinguish blade velocity from the force on the air.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Pitched blades deflect air and create a pressure difference. The downward airflow is not the tangential velocity of a blade tip.

Part c · 3 marks

Explain with a diagram why rain hits the front window but not the rear in this setup.

Hint Try it first

Draw three separate arrows for the falling rain, the wind and the car’s motion.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

v⃗rc=v⃗rain+v⃗wind−v⃗car\vec v_{rc}=\vec v_{\rm rain}+\vec v_{\rm wind}-\vec v_{\rm car}

Subtract the car velocity from the ground velocity of the rain. The relative horizontal component points backwards and the vertical component down: rain approaches the front.

Part d · 4 marks

Will holding an umbrella at 40° from vertical protect a person on the roof?

Hint Try it first

Check which wind speed is actually given. The direction alone does not determine the apparent angle.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

θ=tan⁡−14.62+28\theta=\tan^{-1}\frac{4.62+2}{8}

The book assumes a headwind of 2 m/s, a value missing from the stem. Then the tilt is arctan((4.62+2)/8)≈39.6°, close to 40°. Without wind speed a unique angle cannot be calculated.

Source notes and corrections

The wind speed of 2 m/s used to obtain 39.6° is additional information from the answer, not a stated datum. Correct relative velocity subtracts the car velocity.
