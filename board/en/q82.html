Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define curl.

Hint Try it first

Imagine a tiny paddle wheel in the field.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Curl, ∇×V⃗\nabla\times\vec V, measures local circulation per unit area. Its direction is the local axis of rotation given by the right-hand rule.

Part b · 2 marks

Is a reciprocal vector collinear with its original?

Hint Try it first

Compare directions rather than magnitudes.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Yes. The reciprocal is a positive scalar multiple of the nonzero original, so their lines are parallel.

Part c · 3 marks

Calculate the divergence of the curl of A.

Hint Try it first

Take the divergence of the curl, not the curl alone.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∇⋅(∇×A⃗)=0\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vec A)=0

Divergence of a curl vanishes for these smooth polynomial components because mixed derivatives cancel.

Part d · 4 marks

Which of B and C are solenoidal or irrotational? Test both fields.

Hint Try it first

Do not substitute the similar fields from question 38. Check each sign and power before differentiating.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∇×B⃗=(−2z,1,−1),∇×C⃗=(0,2z−3z2,0)\nabla\times\vec B=(-2z,1,-1),\quad\nabla\times\vec C=(0,2z-3z^2,0)

div B=10 and div C=6y+6xz; neither vanishes identically. Curl B=(−2z,1,−1), curl C=(0,2z−3z²,0). Neither field is everywhere irrotational.

Source notes and corrections

Do not copy the result from question 38: the signs and powers in the given fields differ. Curl zero at isolated points does not make a field irrotational throughout space.
