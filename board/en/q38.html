Part a · 1 mark

What is the centre of gravity?

Hint Try it first

Replace distributed weights by one resultant.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is the point through which the resultant weight acts.

Part b · 2 marks

What does a gravitational potential of 12 J/kg mean?

Hint Try it first

Consider energy per unit test mass.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Relative to the chosen reference, each kilogram at the point has 12 J of gravitational potential energy. The numerical sign depends on the reference convention.

Part c · 3 marks

Find grad p at (2,−1,−2).

Hint Try it first

Differentiate p before substituting numerical coordinates.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∇p=(2y4−2xz,8xy3,−x2)\nabla p=(2y^4-2xz,8xy^3,-x^2)

The gradient is (2y4−2xz,8xy3,−x2)(2y^4-2xz,8xy^3,-x^2); substitution gives (10,−16,−4).

Part d · 4 marks

Which field is solenoidal and which is irrotational?

Hint Try it first

A divergence tests one condition; a curl tests the other. Decide which operation produces a scalar.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∇⋅A⃗=2+3−5,∇×B⃗=(0,3z2−3z2,6x−6x)\nabla\cdot\vec A=2+3-5,\quad\nabla\times\vec B=(0,3z^2-3z^2,6x-6x)

div A=2+3−5=0 throughout. The cyclic component differences for curl B cancel identically. A is solenoidal and B irrotational.

Source notes and corrections

The statement that gravitational potential is negative assumes an isolated attractive field with zero at infinity. The sign changes with the reference choice.
