Part b · 2 marks

How can a bird change direction in flight?

Hint Try it first

Consider the direction of the resultant aerodynamic force.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

By tilting its wings or using unequal wing forces, it creates a lateral component of the aerodynamic force. That acceleration changes the direction of flight.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the rain velocity relative to the boatman.

Hint Try it first

Compute the boat’s ground speed before subtracting it from the rain velocity.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

v⃗rb=(8cos⁡60∘−13,−8sin⁡60∘)\vec v_{rb}=(8\cos60^\circ-13,-8\sin60^\circ)

The boat’s ground speed is 13 m/s. In its vertical plane of motion the relative rain components are 8 cos60°−13=−9 and −8 sin60°≈−6.93 m/s. Relative speed is about 11.36 m/s.

Part d · 4 marks

Will an umbrella tilted at 45° to vertical align with the apparent rain?

Hint Try it first

Compare the ratio of apparent horizontal to downward rain speed with tan 45°.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

θ=tan⁡−198sin⁡60∘\theta=\tan^{-1}\frac{9}{8\sin60^\circ}

The required forward tilt is arctan(9/6.93)≈52.4°. A 45° tilt does not point directly into the rain.

Source notes and corrections

In this problem “ineffective vector” means the null vector. The numerical rain-angle solution assumes the rain lies in the vertical plane of the boat’s ground track.
