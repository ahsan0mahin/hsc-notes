Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is Laplace’s equation?

Hint Try it first

Recall the sum of second spatial derivatives.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

In a source-free region the scalar potential satisfies ∇2ϕ=0\nabla^2\phi=0.

Part b · 2 marks

Why does vertical rain wet the front of a moving vehicle rather than its rear vertical window?

Hint Try it first

Subtract the vehicle’s velocity.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

In the vehicle frame the drops have a backward horizontal component as well as a downward one. They approach the front; the rear is sheltered in this geometry.

Part c · 3 marks

Evaluate ∇⋅(∇×V⃗)\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vec V).

Hint Try it first

Expand the three terms of the divergence after taking the curl. Pair mixed derivatives in opposite orders.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∂x(∂yVz−∂zVy)+∂y(∂zVx−∂xVz)+∂z(∂xVy−∂yVx)=0\partial_x(\partial_yV_z-\partial_zV_y)+\partial_y(\partial_zV_x-\partial_xV_z)+\partial_z(\partial_xV_y-\partial_yV_x)=0

Expanding the divergence of the curl gives six mixed second derivatives. For continuous second derivatives they cancel in opposite pairs, leaving zero.

Part d · 4 marks

Is the curl of the gradient of ϕ\phi zero? Show why.

Hint Try it first

Take the gradient first. Examine the two mixed derivatives in each component of its curl.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

(∇×∇ϕ)x=∂y∂zϕ−∂z∂yϕ=0(\nabla\times\nabla\phi)_x=\partial_y\partial_z\phi-\partial_z\partial_y\phi=0

For example, the x component is ∂y∂zϕ−∂z∂yϕ=0\partial_y\partial_z\phi-\partial_z\partial_y\phi=0. Cyclic permutation gives zero for the other two components as well.

Source notes and corrections

Both identities require sufficient smoothness to interchange the mixed second partial derivatives.
