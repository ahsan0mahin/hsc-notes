Part a · 1 mark

What is a null vector?

Hint Try it first

Imagine coincident start and end points.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Its magnitude is zero and it has no uniquely defined direction.

Part b · 2 marks

Explain the physical significance of divergence.

Hint Try it first

Compare flow into and out of a small volume.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

For a velocity field positive divergence indicates net local outflow, negative divergence net inflow, and zero divergence no net volume flux per unit volume.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the angle between P and the positive x-axis.

Hint Try it first

Use the x component divided by the full length; avoid rounding before taking arccos.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

cos⁡θx=24+4+1\cos\theta_x=\frac{2}{\sqrt{4+4+1}}

The direction cosine is Px/∥P∥=2/3P_x/\lVert P\rVert=2/3, so the angle is about 48.19°.

Part d · 4 marks

Can the scalar product equal the magnitude of the cross product? Find the possible values of a.

Hint Try it first

Equate the signed dot product to the magnitude of the cross product. Check both branches of the absolute value.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

P⃗×Q⃗=(2a+2,−2a−2,0),P⃗⋅Q⃗=8−a\vec P\times\vec Q=(2a+2,-2a-2,0),\quad \vec P\cdot\vec Q=8-a

Solve 8−a=22 ∣a+1∣8-a=2\sqrt2\,\lvert a+1\rvert. The two branches give a=(8−22)/(1+22)a=(8-2\sqrt2)/(1+2\sqrt2) or a=(8+22)/(1−22)a=(8+2\sqrt2)/(1-2\sqrt2). Both satisfy the original equation. Numerically the two values are approximately 1.35 and −5.92.

Source notes and corrections

Rounding 2/3 to 0.67 before taking arccos changes the angle. Keep the absolute value of a+1 in the cross-product magnitude or the negative branch of the solution is lost.
