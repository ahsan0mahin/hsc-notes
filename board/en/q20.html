Question and stimulus

In triangle MNL let A=OL=(−1,−3,2), B=ON=(2,−2,−2) and C=OM perpendicular to both. P=NL, Q=LM and R=NM are directed sides.

  1. (a)

    Define a localized vector.

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Why is work scalar although force and displacement are vectors?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find a unit vector along C.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Use the diagram to verify P+Q=R.

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

Around the triangle, NL=P, LM=Q and NM=R. From the interior point O, the three arrows OL, ON and OM represent A, B and C respectively.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define a localized vector.

Hint Try it first

Can you move its tail freely?

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Its point of application is specified; moving it can change its physical meaning.

Part b · 2 marks

Why is work scalar although force and displacement are vectors?

Hint Try it first

Consider the component of force parallel to displacement.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Work is their dot product Fscos⁡θFs\cos\theta. It is signed but has no spatial direction.

Part c · 3 marks

Find a unit vector along C.

Hint Try it first

Find both possible unit normals using A×B. Perpendicularity alone does not fix the sign.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

C^=±A⃗×B⃗∣A⃗×B⃗∣\hat C=\pm\frac{\vec A\times\vec B}{|\vec A\times\vec B|}

A×B=(10,2,8). The two possible unit normals are ±(5,1,4)/√42; the book selects the negative sense for C.

Part d · 4 marks

Use the diagram to verify P+Q=R.

Hint Try it first

Express each triangle side as the difference of two position vectors from O.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

(A⃗−B⃗)+(C⃗−A⃗)=C⃗−B⃗(\vec A-\vec B)+(\vec C-\vec A)=\vec C-\vec B

P=A−B and Q=C−A. Adding cancels A, giving C−B=R, the displacement from N to M.

Source notes and corrections

Perpendicularity fixes the normal line but not the length or sign of C. The head-to-tail proof of (d) does not need the book’s extra assumption about that magnitude.
