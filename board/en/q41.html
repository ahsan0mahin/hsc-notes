Question and stimulus

A boat starts at A at 12 km/h on a river 4 km wide, with a 6 km/h current. A person at B directly opposite waits for 20 min from departure.

  1. (a)

    What is an area vector?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Does a zero vector have a definite direction?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find the resultant speed when the boat heads along AB.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Can the boatman meet the waiting person at B?

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

AB is the width. The 12 km/h boat arrow points along AB, and the 6 km/h current along the bank. The meeting point B is fixed on land.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is an area vector?

Hint Try it first

A plane admits a normal direction.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Its magnitude is the surface area and its direction is the chosen unit normal.

Part b · 2 marks

Does a zero vector have a definite direction?

Hint Try it first

Imagine its tail and head coinciding.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

No. Its projection is zero on every axis and it cannot be normalized to a direction vector.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the resultant speed when the boat heads along AB.

Hint Try it first

Treat aiming squarely across as the initial condition for this calculation.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

R=122+62R=\sqrt{12^2+6^2}

The perpendicular velocities give √(12²+6²)=6√5 km/h.

Part d · 4 marks

Can the boatman meet the waiting person at B?

Hint Try it first

For a meeting at B, first change the heading to cancel drift. Then compare the arrival time with the deadline.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

tB=60(4)122−62 mint_B=\frac{60(4)}{\sqrt{12^2-6^2}}\,\mathrm{min}

To land at B the effective crossing speed is √108 km/h. The time is about 23.1 min, so the person has left.

Source notes and corrections

Horizontal velocity cancellation determines the heading without assigning a numerical value to tan 90°.
