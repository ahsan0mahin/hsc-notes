Original question 1 · Collection 45

Question and stimulus

A boat heads perpendicular to the banks at 10 m/s relative to water. The river is 400 m wide and flows at 2 m/s.

  1. (a)

    What is a null vector?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Why does a tightrope walker carry a pole?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find the time to cross.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    How far and in what direction from A will the boat land?

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

A is the starting point, B directly opposite it, C downstream. The angle θ is measured between AC and the bank, not between AC and AB.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is a null vector?

Hint Try it first

Imagine coincident start and end points.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Its magnitude is zero and it has no uniquely defined direction.

Part b · 2 marks

Why does a tightrope walker carry a pole?

Hint Try it first

Consider the response to a disturbing torque.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

The pole spreads mass farther from the rotation axis and increases the moment of inertia. A given torque then produces less angular acceleration, allowing more time to correct the tilt.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the time to cross.

Hint Try it first

The perpendicular component is already known; the current acts along the shore.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

t=400/10=40 st=400/10=40\,\mathrm s

Only the 10 m/s component covers the width. Time=400/10=40 s.

Part d · 4 marks

How far and in what direction from A will the boat land?

Hint Try it first

Calculate drift over the time found in (c). Combine drift and width as perpendicular displacements.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

AC=4002+802,tan⁡θ=400/80AC=\sqrt{400^2+80^2},\quad\tan\theta=400/80

Drift is 2×40=80 m. AC=√(400²+80²)≈408 m at arctan(400/80)≈78.69° to the downstream bank.

Source notes and corrections

The angle follows from arctan(5). A balancing pole need not lower the centre of gravity; increasing rotational inertia is an independent benefit.
