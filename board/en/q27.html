Question and stimulus

Two swimmers race across a 1 km wide river in a 3 km/h current. Both swim at 6 km/h. The first makes 60° with the upstream direction; the second heads perpendicular to the current.

  1. (a)

    What is angular momentum?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    When can work be zero?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find the first swimmer’s resultant velocity.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Which swimmer crosses first?

    4 marks

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is angular momentum?

Hint Try it first

Measure position from the point about which rotation is considered.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Angular momentum is the moment of linear momentum, L⃗=r⃗×p⃗\vec L=\vec r\times\vec p.

Part b · 2 marks

When can work be zero?

Hint Try it first

Examine the factors in the scalar product.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

For constant force, W=Fs cos θ vanishes for zero force, zero displacement or mutually perpendicular force and displacement.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the first swimmer’s resultant velocity.

Hint Try it first

Measure the first heading from downstream before adding the current.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

(vx,vy)=(3−6cos⁡60∘,6sin⁡60∘)(v_x,v_y)=(3-6\cos60^\circ,6\sin60^\circ)

The horizontal component is 3−6 cos60°=0. The resultant is 6 sin60°=3√3 km/h straight across.

Part d · 4 marks

Which swimmer crosses first?

Hint Try it first

Use crossing components, not resultant speeds, to compare the times.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

t1=133>16=t2t_1=\frac{1}{3\sqrt3}>\frac16=t_2

Times are 1/(3√3) and 1/6 h. The second swimmer arrives earlier, though not directly opposite.

Source notes and corrections

A zero downstream component means a resultant perpendicular to the current. There is no need to divide by zero to find its angle.
