Question and stimulus

Three forces act simultaneously on a 5 kg mass: 8 N north, 4 N south and 3 N east. The second figure shows acceleration at 53.13° north of east.

  1. (a)

    Define the position (radius) vector.

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Why use a long rope to tow a boat from the bank?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find the net force magnitude in figure 1.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Is the acceleration shown in figure 2 correct?

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

At m the north and south forces oppose each other; the third force points east. Compare the net force direction in figure 1 with the acceleration in figure 2.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define the position (radius) vector.

Hint Try it first

Choose an origin first.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is the directed displacement from the chosen origin O to the point P, r⃗=OP→\vec r=\overrightarrow{OP}.

Part b · 2 marks

Why use a long rope to tow a boat from the bank?

Hint Try it first

Compare the rope angle at a fixed lateral separation.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

A longer rope can make a smaller angle to the desired direction. More of the tension then acts along the river rather than pulling towards the bank.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the net force magnitude in figure 1.

Hint Try it first

Add northward and southward forces algebraically before combining with the eastward force.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

F=32+(8−4)2 NF=\sqrt{3^2+(8-4)^2}\,\mathrm N

Net force is 4 N north and 3 N east; magnitude √(4²+3²)=5 N.

Part d · 4 marks

Is the acceleration shown in figure 2 correct?

Hint Try it first

Use both the magnitude and the direction of the acceleration, not just F/m.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

a=5/5,θ=tan⁡−1(4/3)a=5/5,\quad\theta=\tan^{-1}(4/3)

Acceleration is 5/5=1 m/s², at arctan(4/3)=53.13° north of east, agreeing with the figure.

Source notes and corrections

North is upwards on the drawing, not vertically upwards in the gravitational sense. The force magnitude uses both squared components.
