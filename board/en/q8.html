Part a · 1 mark

Define the position (radius) vector.

Hint Try it first

Choose an origin first.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is the directed displacement from the chosen origin O to the point P, r⃗=OP→\vec r=\overrightarrow{OP}.

Part b · 2 marks

Why is a position vector localized?

Hint Try it first

Change the tail without changing the arrow.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It begins at a specified origin. Moving its tail would no longer locate the same point relative to that origin.

Part c · 3 marks

Evaluate the divergence of V at (1,−1,1).

Hint Try it first

Differentiate the x, y and z components with respect to their matching coordinates before substituting the point.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∇⋅V⃗=3yz2+4xy−x3y2\nabla\cdot\vec V=3yz^2+4xy-x^3y^2

Differentiate matching components: 3yz2+4xy−x3y2=−83yz^2+4xy-x^3y^2=-8 at the given point.

Part d · 4 marks

If A represents a diameter, can B represent a tangent at its endpoint?

Hint Try it first

What angle should the radius make with a tangent at its endpoint? Test the angle using A and B.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

A⃗⋅B⃗=9(4)+1(−6)+(−6)(5)=0\vec A\cdot\vec B=9(4)+1(-6)+(-6)(5)=0

A·B=36−6−30=0. Their directions are perpendicular, so B has the required tangent direction at an endpoint of that diameter in the plane of the circle.

Source notes and corrections

Perpendicularity determines the tangent direction. A tangent line must also pass through the appropriate point of contact.
