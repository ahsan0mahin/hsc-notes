Question and stimulus

The position vectors of A, B and C are (1,2,−1), (3,−1,2) and (2,5,−4).

  1. (a)

    Define the position (radius) vector.

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find the angle between AB and AC.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Are the points collinear or merely coplanar?

    4 marks

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define the position (radius) vector.

Hint Try it first

Choose an origin first.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is the directed displacement from the chosen origin O to the point P, r⃗=OP→\vec r=\overrightarrow{OP}.

Part b · 2 marks

Explain the physical significance of divergence.

Hint Try it first

Compare flow into and out of a small volume.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

For a velocity field positive divergence indicates net local outflow, negative divergence net inflow, and zero divergence no net volume flux per unit volume.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the angle between AB and AC.

Hint Try it first

Both arrows must begin at A. Find their dot product and their two lengths.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

cos⁡θ=−162219\cos\theta=\frac{-16}{\sqrt{22}\sqrt{19}}

AB=(2,−3,3), AC=(1,3,−3). Their dot product is −16. Divide by √22 times √19 and take arccos: about 141.5°.

Part d · 4 marks

Are the points collinear or merely coplanar?

Hint Try it first

Check if one displacement is a scalar multiple of the other.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

(2,−3,3)×(1,3,−3)=(0,9,9)≠0⃗(2,-3,3)\times(1,3,-3)=(0,9,9)\ne\vec0

The two displacement vectors are not proportional, so the points are not collinear. Three noncollinear points determine a plane.

Source notes and corrections

AB and AC start at the same vertex. The nonzero cross product excludes collinearity; the three points still determine a plane.
