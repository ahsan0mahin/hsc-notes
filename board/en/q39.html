Question and stimulus

A 1.5 km wide river flows at 4 m/s; a boat moves at 6 m/s relative to water. In diagram A it aims upstream and lands directly opposite. In B it aims across and drifts downstream.

  1. (a)

    What is a null vector?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Explain why the angle between the negative and reciprocal of a nonzero vector is fixed.

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Calculate the resultant speed in diagram A.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Which crossing takes less time?

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

In the first sketch v points upstream and R points across; in the second v points across and R downstream of the perpendicular. Both sketches have the same current and width.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is a null vector?

Hint Try it first

Imagine coincident start and end points.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Its magnitude is zero and it has no uniquely defined direction.

Part b · 2 marks

Explain why the angle between the negative and reciprocal of a nonzero vector is fixed.

Hint Try it first

Draw both relative to the original arrow.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

The negative points opposite the original; the reciprocal points along it. The angle between them is always 180°.

Part c · 3 marks

Calculate the resultant speed in diagram A.

Hint Try it first

In sketch A the boat must spend part of its speed cancelling the current.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

R=62−42 m/sR=\sqrt{6^2-4^2}\,\mathrm{m/s}

The upstream component cancels 4 m/s. The crossing component is √(6²−4²)=√20≈4.47 m/s.

Part d · 4 marks

Which crossing takes less time?

Hint Try it first

Use the across-river component from each sketch and the same width of 1500 m.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

ta=1500/20,tb=1500/6t_a=1500/\sqrt{20},\quad t_b=1500/6

A takes 1500/√20≈335.4 s and B takes 1500/6=250 s. B is quicker.

Source notes and corrections

The negative vector is opposite the original while the reciprocal has the original direction. Do not confuse their angles relative to the original with their angle to one another.
