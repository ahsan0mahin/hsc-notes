Question and stimulus

A farmer’s parallelogram has adjacent sides P=(4,−4,1) and Q=(2,−2,−1). Someone plans a three-dimensional frame with A=(3,−4,5), B=(2,−1,1), C=(1,2,−3).

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define curl.

Hint Try it first

Imagine a tiny paddle wheel in the field.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Curl, ∇×V⃗\nabla\times\vec V, measures local circulation per unit area. Its direction is the local axis of rotation given by the right-hand rule.

Part b · 2 marks

Can a vector differential operator turn a scalar into a vector?

Hint Try it first

Consider the spatial derivatives.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Yes. The gradient of a scalar field combines its partial derivatives along each axis into a vector pointing towards the steepest increase.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the land area using its diagonals.

Hint Try it first

Form the two diagonals P+Q and P−Q. Their cross product gives twice the area.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Δ=12∣(P⃗+Q⃗)×(P⃗−Q⃗)∣\Delta=\tfrac12|(\vec P+\vec Q)\times(\vec P-\vec Q)|

The diagonals are (6,−6,0) and (2,−2,2). Half the magnitude of their cross product is 6√2 square units.

Part d · 4 marks

Can the proposed frame span three dimensions?

Hint Try it first

A frame with volume needs more than three nonzero edge lengths. Test the scalar triple product.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

(3,−4,5)⋅(1,7,5)=0(3,-4,5)\cdot(1,7,5)=0

B×C=(1,7,5) and A·(B×C)=3−28+25=0. The volume vanishes; the frame is planar.

Source notes and corrections

The exact area is 6√2 square units. A zero scalar triple product gives zero volume for the proposed adjacent edges.
