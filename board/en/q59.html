Part a · 1 mark

What is weightlessness?

Hint Try it first

Consider the reading on a weighing scale.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is zero apparent weight: there is no supporting reaction, even though gravity may still act.

Part b · 2 marks

Compare the speeds of satellites of different masses in the same circular orbit.

Hint Try it first

Balance gravitational and centripetal force.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

GMm/r2=mv2/rGMm/r^2=mv^2/r gives v=GM/rv=\sqrt{GM/r}. At the same orbital radius the speeds are equal, independent of satellite mass.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the angle between A and B.

Hint Try it first

Full solution Diagram and explanation

cos⁡θ=2+2−3146\cos\theta=\frac{2+2-3}{\sqrt{14}\sqrt6}

A·B=1, so cos θ=1/√84 and θ≈83.74°.

Part d · 4 marks

Can these three vectors serve as the edges of a rectangular solid?

Hint Try it first

One nonperpendicular pair is enough to exclude a rectangular solid.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

A⃗⋅B⃗=1≠0\vec A\cdot\vec B=1\ne0

Adjacent edges of a rectangular solid must be pairwise perpendicular. A·B is nonzero, which already rules this out.

Source notes and corrections

Satellite speed at a given circular orbital radius does not specify velocity direction. The rectangular-solid test assumes the vectors are adjacent edges.
