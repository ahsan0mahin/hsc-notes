Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define curl.

Hint Try it first

Imagine a tiny paddle wheel in the field.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Curl, ∇×V⃗\nabla\times\vec V, measures local circulation per unit area. Its direction is the local axis of rotation given by the right-hand rule.

Part b · 2 marks

When is the resultant of three vectors zero?

Hint Try it first

Trace all three in order.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

They sum to zero when placed head to tail in a closed triangle, so the final head returns to the starting point.

Part c · 3 marks

Show that equal pulls bisect the angle between the ropes.

Hint Try it first

Construct the parallelogram of the equal pulls and compare the triangles on either side of the diagonal.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

tan⁡β=Fsin⁡αF+Fcos⁡α=tan⁡(α/2)\tan\beta=\frac{F\sin\alpha}{F+F\cos\alpha}=\tan(\alpha/2)

The equal pulls form adjacent sides of a rhombus. Its diagonal, the resultant, bisects the angle.

Part d · 4 marks

How should they pull to maximize the resultant for a given effort?

Hint Try it first

In the expression for R² only the angular term changes if the force magnitudes are fixed.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

R2=P2+Q2+2PQcos⁡α≤(P+Q)2R^2=P^2+Q^2+2PQ\cos\alpha\le(P+Q)^2

R2=P2+Q2+2PQcos⁡αR^2=P^2+Q^2+2PQ\cos\alpha. For fixed forces cos α must be maximal: pull parallel in the same direction.

Source notes and corrections

Exclude the degenerate case of equal and exactly opposite pulls when assigning a bisector direction: a zero resultant has none.
