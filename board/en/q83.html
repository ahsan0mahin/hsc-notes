Question and stimulus

A boat heads along OP at 2.3 m/s on a 1.4 km wide river flowing at 1.1 m/s and reaches Q. Rain begins falling vertically at 4.2 m/s.

Reading the diagram

OP is the width and OQ the ground track. In the rain problem both of those directions are horizontal in space; the falling rain is vertical.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is divergence?

Hint Try it first

Consider the flux through the faces of a tiny closed box.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Divergence is the net outward flux per unit volume in the limit of a vanishing volume: ∇⋅V⃗\nabla\cdot\vec V.

Part b · 2 marks

Why is a trolley handle long?

Hint Try it first

At a fixed hand height compare the angle to the ground.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

A longer handle can make a smaller angle to the horizontal. The same pull then has a greater forward component Fcos⁡θF\cos\theta.

Part c · 3 marks

At what angle from vertical should the boatman hold his umbrella?

Hint Try it first

Combine the two horizontal boat components before comparing them with vertical rain speed.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

tan⁡θ=1.12+2.324.2\tan\theta=\frac{\sqrt{1.1^2+2.3^2}}{4.2}

Boat ground speed is √(2.3²+1.1²)≈2.55 m/s. Tilt forward from vertical by arctan(2.55/4.2)≈31.3°.

Part d · 4 marks

Would heading along OQ at the same speed shorten the crossing time?

Hint Try it first

The starting heading is already normal to the shore. Compare the new heading’s perpendicular component.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

14002.3cos⁡25.56∘>14002.3\frac{1400}{2.3\cos25.56^\circ}>\frac{1400}{2.3}

OP is perpendicular to the banks, already maximizing crossing speed. Aiming along OQ at angle θ to OP reduces the across component to 2.3 cos θ, so it takes longer.

Source notes and corrections

Treat both boat velocity components as horizontal and the rain velocity as vertical. In three axes the relative velocity is (−1.1,−2.3,−4.2) m/s.
