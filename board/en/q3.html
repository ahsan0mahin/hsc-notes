Part a · 1 mark

Define a unit vector.

Hint Try it first

Think about direction separately from length.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

A unit vector has magnitude one. Along a nonzero vector A it is A⃗/∣A⃗∣\vec A/|\vec A|.

Part b · 2 marks

Why is divergence a scalar?

Hint Try it first

Look at how del combines with the field.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

The dot product adds the three component derivatives into one signed number. It gives net outward flow, not a direction.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the unit vector along PQ→×QR→\overrightarrow{PQ}\times\overrightarrow{QR}.

Hint Try it first

Form Q−P and R−Q before crossing them. What must you divide the result by?

Full solution Diagram and explanation

n^=PQ→×QR→∣PQ→×QR→∣=−19i^−20j^+3k^770\hat n=\frac{\overrightarrow{PQ}\times\overrightarrow{QR}}{|\overrightarrow{PQ}\times\overrightarrow{QR}|}=\frac{-19\hat i-20\hat j+3\hat k}{\sqrt{770}}

PQ=(−6,6,2), QR=(1,−2,−7). Their cross product is (−38,−40,6); divide by its length to get (−19,−20,3)/770(−19,−20,3)/\sqrt{770}.

Part d · 4 marks

Do the points form a right triangle? Show your calculation.

Hint Try it first

Compare the largest squared side with the sum of the other two.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

54+66≠7654+66\ne76

The squared lengths PQ², QR² and PR² are 76, 54 and 66. No two add to the third, so the triangle is not right-angled.

Source notes and corrections

The printed z component of PR is inconsistent with its subtraction. PR=(−5,4,−5) and its length is √66.
