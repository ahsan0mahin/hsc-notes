Question and stimulus

In a 4 km/h current, Sohel steers along AB at 8 km/h and reaches D directly opposite A in 10 min. Rohan steers along AD at 10 km/h but lands downstream at C.

  1. (a)

    Define the position (radius) vector.

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Explain why the parallelogram law obeys commutation.

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find Sohel’s resultant speed.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Is Rohan’s downstream distance DC equal to the width AD?

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

From A, AB points upstream of AD, the perpendicular across the river. AC is the diagonal path downstream. DC measures drift along the far bank, not distance travelled by the boat.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define the position (radius) vector.

Hint Try it first

Choose an origin first.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is the directed displacement from the chosen origin O to the point P, r⃗=OP→\vec r=\overrightarrow{OP}.

Part b · 2 marks

Explain why the parallelogram law obeys commutation.

Hint Try it first

Exchange the adjacent sides.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

The same diagonal joins the common tail to the opposite corner in either order: A+B=B+A.

Part c · 3 marks

Find Sohel’s resultant speed.

Hint Try it first

Sohel’s upstream component cancels the whole current. Use his remaining component.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

R=82−42 km/hR=\sqrt{8^2-4^2}\,\mathrm{km/h}

Sohel cancels the current. His crossing speed is √(8²−4²)=4√3 km/h.

Part d · 4 marks

Is Rohan’s downstream distance DC equal to the width AD?

Hint Try it first

Find AD using Sohel’s time. Rohan’s crossing time uses his own across-bank speed; apply the drift only for that time.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

AD=1060R,DC=4AD10AD=\frac{10}{60}R,\quad DC=4\frac{AD}{10}

AD=(10/60)4√3 km. Rohan crosses in AD/10 hours and drifts DC=4AD/10. Thus DC is only 40% of the width.

Source notes and corrections

DC is the downstream component of Rohan’s displacement, not the length of his diagonal route AC.
