Question and stimulus

Black, blue and red marbles on a flat table have velocities (2,1,−1), (3,−2,−4) and (1,−3,5) respectively and collide.

  1. (a)

    What is epoch in simple harmonic motion?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Why does a centripetal force do no work?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Explain the impulse if the red marble stops after collision.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Can the marbles remain on a flat table? Examine the stated velocity data.

    4 marks

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is epoch in simple harmonic motion?

Hint Try it first

Write the phase as a function of time.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is the initial phase, the phase angle at time zero.

Part b · 2 marks

Why does a centripetal force do no work?

Hint Try it first

Draw the instantaneous displacement tangent to the circle.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

The force is radial and perpendicular to the displacement, so its dot product with the displacement is zero.

Part c · 3 marks

Explain the impulse if the red marble stops after collision.

Hint Try it first

Write the impulse as the change from initial to final momentum for the red marble.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

J⃗3=m3(0⃗−v⃗3)\vec J_3=m_3(\vec0-\vec v_3)

If it stops, its change in momentum is zero minus its initial momentum. The net impulse must therefore be equal and opposite to its initial momentum.

Part d · 4 marks

Can the marbles remain on a flat table? Examine the stated velocity data.

Hint Try it first

Test the flat-table assumption for the initial velocities before predicting the final motion.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

(v⃗1×v⃗2)⋅v⃗3=−56(\vec v_1\times\vec v_2)\cdot\vec v_3=-56

The scalar triple product of the given velocities is −56, not zero. They cannot even describe three initial velocities confined to one plane. The final velocities cannot be deduced from these inconsistent data alone.

Source notes and corrections

The triple product tests the initial velocities, not the individual final velocities. Momentum conservation alone cannot prove where each marble goes after the collision.
