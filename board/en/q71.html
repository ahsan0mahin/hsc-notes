Question and stimulus

A river is 5 km wide and flows at 8 km/h. Two boats travel at 12 km/h relative to water: the first along AE at 45° to downstream, the second along AC at 65° to upstream.

  1. (a)

    Define displacement vector.

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    If rotating fan blades suddenly shorten, what happens to the rpm?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find the horizontal distance travelled by the first boat.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Does the second boat cross by the shortest path?

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

AE is 45° to downstream and AC is 65° to upstream. Use one common downstream axis before adding the current to each boat velocity.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define displacement vector.

Hint Try it first

Compare endpoints rather than the route between them.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is final position minus initial position: Δr⃗=r⃗f−r⃗i\Delta\vec r=\vec r_f-\vec r_i.

Part b · 2 marks

If rotating fan blades suddenly shorten, what happens to the rpm?

Hint Try it first

Consider angular momentum during the short change.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

If mass moves inward and external torque is negligible, the moment of inertia falls. With Iω conserved the angular speed and rpm rise.

Part c · 3 marks

Find the horizontal distance travelled by the first boat.

Hint Try it first

The along-bank drift accumulates throughout the time needed to cover the width.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

x=5(8+12cos⁡45∘)12sin⁡45∘x=\frac{5(8+12\cos45^\circ)}{12\sin45^\circ}

Time=5/(12 sin45°) h. Downstream displacement=(8+12 cos45°) times that time, about 9.71 km.

Part d · 4 marks

Does the second boat cross by the shortest path?

Hint Try it first

Measure the second heading from downstream before testing horizontal cancellation.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

vx=8+12cos⁡115∘≠0v_x=8+12\cos115^\circ\ne0

Net downstream speed is 8−12 cos65°≈2.93 km/h. It is not zero, so the path is not the shortest.

Source notes and corrections

Conservation of angular momentum for the fan assumes negligible external torque during the change. For the second boat the heading is 115° to downstream.
