Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

Define the position (radius) vector.

Hint Try it first

Choose an origin first.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is the directed displacement from the chosen origin O to the point P, r⃗=OP→\vec r=\overrightarrow{OP}.

Part b · 2 marks

Explain why j^×k^\hat j\times\hat k is a unit vector.

Hint Try it first

Consider the angle between the axes.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

The magnitude is 1×1×sin90°=1 and the cyclic order gives the +x direction.

Part c · 3 marks

Find a so the field r is solenoidal.

Hint Try it first

Set the sum of matching component derivatives to the solenoidal condition.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∂x(ax)+∂yy+∂z2=a+1\partial_x(ax)+\partial_y y+\partial_z2=a+1

Divergence is a+1. Set it to zero: a=−1.

Part d · 4 marks

Is the field P×Q rotational or irrotational?

Hint Try it first

Check whether any component of P×Q actually depends on position.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

P⃗×Q⃗=(−19,18,−4),∇×(P⃗×Q⃗)=0⃗\vec P\times\vec Q=(-19,18,-4),\quad\nabla\times(\vec P\times\vec Q)=\vec0

P and Q are constant, so every component of their cross product is constant. Its curl vanishes everywhere.

Source notes and corrections

A curl-free field need not be globally conservative on an arbitrary domain. Here the cross product is constant and is conservative as well.
