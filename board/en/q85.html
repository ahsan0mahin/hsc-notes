Question and stimulus

While stationary Shanta sees rain arriving from the south at 8 m/s, 40° from vertical. She then walks north and holds her umbrella backwards at 20° from vertical.

  1. (a)

    What is a fundamental unit?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    Why does a rotating ceiling fan move air downward?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    What is the true rain speed?

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Test the claim that Shanta walks more slowly than the true rain speed.

    4 marks

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is a fundamental unit?

Hint Try it first

Is the unit derived from other units in the system?

Full solution Diagram and explanation

It is an independently chosen unit for a base quantity, such as the metre for length.

Part b · 2 marks

Why does a rotating ceiling fan move air downward?

Hint Try it first

Distinguish blade velocity from the force on the air.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Pitched blades deflect air and create a pressure difference. The downward airflow is not the tangential velocity of a blade tip.

Part c · 3 marks

What is the true rain speed?

Hint Try it first

Which reference frame does a stationary observer share?

Full solution Diagram and explanation

vground=8 m/sv_{\rm ground}=8\,\mathrm{m/s}

She initially observes from rest, so the observed 8 m/s is the ground-frame speed.

Part d · 4 marks

Test the claim that Shanta walks more slowly than the true rain speed.

Hint Try it first

The backward tilt determines the sign of the relative horizontal component. Use that sign when recovering the walking speed.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

vS=8sin⁡40∘−8cos⁡40∘tan⁡20∘v_S=8\sin40^\circ-8\cos40^\circ\tan20^\circ

Northward rain speed is 8 sin40°, downward speed 8 cos40°. The remaining northward component equals 8 cos40° tan20°. Her speed is their difference, about 2.91 m/s, below 8 m/s.

Source notes and corrections

The backwards umbrella tilt specifies which side the apparent rain comes from. An unsigned 20° angle alone would leave another algebraic branch.
