Part a · 1 mark

Define collinear vectors.

Hint Try it first

Compare lines of action, not lengths.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

They lie along the same line or parallel lines; their senses may agree or oppose each other.

Part b · 2 marks

Can two vectors have equal sum and difference?

Hint Try it first

Distinguish vector equality from equal magnitudes.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Vector equality A+B=A−B requires B=0. Equal magnitudes instead require A·B=0, as follows by squaring both magnitudes.

Part c · 3 marks

Find a vector normal to the plane of A and B.

Hint Try it first

Use the right-hand rule to choose the order of A and B.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

A⃗×B⃗=(24−1,−1−18,−3−4)\vec A\times\vec B=(24-1,-1-18,-3-4)

A×B=(23,−19,−7), perpendicular to both vectors.

Part d · 4 marks

Does the field C act as a fluid source at (2,−1,2)?

Hint Try it first

First calculate divergence symbolically, then substitute the coordinates and inspect the sign.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

∇⋅C⃗∣(2,−1,2)=−16−4+16=−4\nabla\cdot\vec C\big|_{(2,-1,2)}=-16-4+16=-4

The divergence is 4xyz+2yz+2x2y2z4xyz+2yz+2x^2y^2z. Substitution gives −4, so the point is a sink rather than a source.

Source notes and corrections

The printed expansion should read 24−1, not 24×1. Equality of vector magnitudes is different from equality of the vectors themselves.
