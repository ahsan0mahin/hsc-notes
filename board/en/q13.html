Question and stimulus

Tamal heads from P along PS at 5 km/h, at 100° to a current of 3 km/h. He lands at M; Q is directly opposite P across a 2 km wide river.

  1. (a)

    What is a vector differential operator?

    1 mark
  2. (b)

    On what does centripetal force depend?

    2 marks
  3. (c)

    Find QM.

    3 marks
  4. (d)

    Compare the time to reach M with the time to go directly from P to Q in still water.

    4 marks

Reading the diagram

Across the parallel banks PQ is perpendicular to the current; M is downstream of Q. PS shows where Tamal points the bow, while PM is the ground track.

Try it yourself

Part a · 1 mark

What is a vector differential operator?

Hint Try it first

Pair each partial derivative with its coordinate direction.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

The del operator is ∇=i^∂x+j^∂y+k^∂z\nabla=\hat i\partial_x+\hat j\partial_y+\hat k\partial_z. It acts on fields rather than representing a displacement.

Part b · 2 marks

On what does centripetal force depend?

Hint Try it first

Relate inward acceleration to the radius.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Its magnitude is mv2/rmv^2/r: proportional to mass and speed squared, inversely proportional to radius.

Part c · 3 marks

Find QM.

Hint Try it first

Find the crossing time from the perpendicular component. Multiply that time by the net downstream speed.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

QM=(3+5cos⁡100∘)25sin⁡100∘ kmQM=(3+5\cos100^\circ)\frac{2}{5\sin100^\circ}\,\mathrm{km}

Crossing time is 2/(5sin⁡100∘)2/(5\sin100^\circ) h. Multiply by the downstream speed 3+5cos⁡100∘3+5\cos100^\circ to get about 0.87 km.

Part d · 4 marks

Compare the time to reach M with the time to go directly from P to Q in still water.

Hint Try it first

Use the same width twice but different perpendicular speeds. Subtract before rounding to seconds.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Δt=3600(25sin⁡100∘−25)s\Delta t=3600\left(\frac{2}{5\sin100^\circ}-\frac25\right)\mathrm s

Without current and aiming at Q, the time is 24 min. At 100° the crossing takes about 24.37 min, roughly 22 s longer (about 21 s with the book’s rounding).

Source notes and corrections

Before rounding, QM is approximately 865.9 m and the difference in crossing time is about 22.2 s.
