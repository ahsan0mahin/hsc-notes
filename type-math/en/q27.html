River–boat and current

Question 27

Dhaka Board · 2023 · Board question no. 1 · book p. 44

To cross a river of width 1 km1\text{ km}, two swimmers take part in a swimming competition. The first swimmer swims with velocity 6 kmh−16\text{ kmh}^{-1} at an angle 60∘60^\circ with the upstream direction, and the second swimmer swims with velocity 6 kmh−16\text{ kmh}^{-1} straight across. The velocity of the current in the river is 3 kmh−13\text{ kmh}^{-1}.

Part c · 3 marks

Determine the resultant velocity of the first swimmer.

Hint Try it first

Measure the first heading from downstream before adding the current.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Here, the velocity of the first swimmer v=6 km h−1v=6\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}; the velocity of the current u=3 km h−1u=3\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}; the angle between the swimmer and the current α=180∘−60∘=120∘\alpha=180^\circ-60^\circ=120^\circ; the resultant velocity of the first swimmer R=?R=?.

The swimmer's velocity v=6 km h−1v=6\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}} from the lower bank towards the upper left. The angle with the upstream horizontal is 60° and with the downstream current α=120∘\alpha=120^\circ. A vertical line along the width of the river is shown.
We know, the magnitude of the resultant velocity,

<div class="math">R=u2+v2+2uvcos⁡θ=32+62+2×3×6cos⁡120∘=9+36−18=33 km h−1.\begin{aligned} R&=\sqrt{u^2+v^2+2uv\cos\theta}\\ &=\sqrt{3^2+6^2+2\times3\times6\cos120^\circ}\\ &=\sqrt{9+36-18}=3\sqrt3\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}. \end{aligned}</div>
Direction of the resultant,

tan⁡θ=vsin⁡αu+vcos⁡α\tan\theta=\frac{v\sin\alpha}{u+v\cos\alpha}
or,

θ=tan⁡−1(6sin⁡120∘3+6cos⁡120∘)\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{6\sin120^\circ}{3+6\cos120^\circ}\right)

θ=90∘.\theta = 90^\circ.
∴ the resultant velocity of the swimmer 33 km h−13\sqrt3\ \mathrm{km\,h^{-1}} at an angle of 90° with the direction of the current. Ans.

Part d · 4 marks

Which swimmer will be able to cross the river first in the said competition? Analyse mathematically.

Hint Try it first

Use crossing components, not resultant speeds, to compare the times.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

In the case of the first swimmer, required time,
t1=dv1sin⁡120∘t_1 = \frac{d}{v_1\sin 120^\circ}

t1=1632t_1 = \frac{1}{6\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}

t1=0.19 hrt_1 = 0.19\text{ hr}
In the case of the second swimmer, required time,
t2=dvsin⁡90∘t_2 = \frac{d}{v\sin 90^\circ}

t2=16t_2 = \frac{1}{6}

t2=0.17 hrt_2 = 0.17\text{ hr}
Since t1>t2t_1 > t_2. Therefore, the second swimmer will be able to cross the river first.
