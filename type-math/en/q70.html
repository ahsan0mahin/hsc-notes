Rashidazzoha Government Women's College, Sirajganj · Board question no. 1 · book p. 66

Three forces act simultaneously on a body of mass m=5kgm = 5\text{kg}, as shown in Figure 1.

Part c · 3 marks

What is the magnitude of the net force acting on the body in Figure 1?

Hint Try it first

Add northward and southward forces algebraically before combining with the eastward force.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Net force in the horizontal direction, Rx=3NR_x = 3\text{N} Net force in the vertical direction, Ry=Fy1−Fy2  ⟹  or, Ry=8−4=4NR_y = F_{y1} - F_{y2} \implies \text{or, } R_y = 8 - 4 = 4\text{N} Magnitude of the net force,
R=Rx2+Ry2R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2}
32+42=25\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25}
∴R=5N Ans.\therefore R = 5\text{N Ans.}

Part d · 4 marks

In the light of Figure-1, verify the correctness of Figure-2.

Hint Try it first

Use both the magnitude and the direction of the acceleration, not just F/m.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Acceleration produced by the net force,
a=Rma = \frac{R}{m}

a=5 N5 kga = \frac{5\text{ N}}{5\text{ kg}}

a=1 ms−2a = 1\text{ ms}^{-2} Let the acceleration make an angle θ\theta with the horizontal (east).
tan⁡θ=RyRx\tan\theta = \frac{R_y}{R_x}

tan⁡θ=43\tan\theta = \frac{4}{3}

tan⁡θ=tan⁡−1(43)\tan\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)

tan⁡θ=53.13∘\tan\theta = 53.13^\circ
Figure-2 shows the acceleration a=1 ms−2a = 1\text{ ms}^{-2} and its direction upwards at an angle 53.13∘53.13^\circ with the horizontal (east). The magnitude of the acceleration we calculated is a=1 ms−2a = 1\text{ ms}^{-2} and the direction θ=53.13∘\theta = 53.13^\circ (upwards). Therefore, the information in Figure-2 is completely correct in the light of Figure-1.
