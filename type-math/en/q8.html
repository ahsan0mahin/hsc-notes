Answer to Question 8

We know that the diameter of a circle is perpendicular to the tangent drawn on the circumference of the circle. If the vectors A⃗\vec{A} and B⃗\vec{B} given in the stem are mutually perpendicular, then the statement given in the question will be correct. Again, we know,
A⃗⋅B⃗=ABcos⁡θ\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = AB\cos\theta
or, cos⁡θ=A⃗⋅B⃗AB\text{or, } \cos\theta = \frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{AB}
or, cos⁡θ=(9i^+j^−6k^)⋅(4i^−6j^+5k^)AB\text{or, } \cos\theta = \frac{(9\hat{i} + \hat{j} - 6\hat{k}) \cdot (4\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 5\hat{k})}{AB}

or, cos⁡θ=0\text{or, } \cos\theta = 0
∴θ=cos⁡−10\therefore \theta = \cos^{-1} 0

θ=90∘\theta = 90^\circ
or, Here,
A⃗=9i^+j^−6k^\vec{A} = 9\hat{i} + \hat{j} - 6\hat{k}
B⃗=4i^−6j^+5k^\vec{B} = 4\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}
Since the diameter of a circle is perpendicular to the tangent drawn on the circumference of the circle, and A⃗\vec{A} and B⃗\vec{B} are situated perpendicular to each other. If ∴A⃗\therefore \vec{A} represents the diameter of a circle, B⃗\vec{B} will represent a tangent drawn on the circumference of that circle.
