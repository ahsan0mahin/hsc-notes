River–boat and current

Question 68

Rajshahi College · Board question no. 1 · book p. 65

A boat set off along the width of the river with velocity 12ms−112\text{ms}^{-1}. The velocity of the current in the river is 5ms−15\text{ms}^{-1}, and because of the wind the rain is falling with velocity 8ms−18\text{ms}^{-1} at an angle 60∘60^\circ with the resultant of the velocities of the boat and the current.

Part c · 3 marks

Determine the velocity of the rain relative to the boatman.

Hint Try it first

Compute the boat’s ground speed before subtracting it from the rain velocity.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Resultant velocity of the boat and the current,
V=vn2+vs2+2vnvscos⁡αV = \sqrt{v_n^2 + v_s^2 + 2v_n v_s \cos\alpha}
or, V=122+52+2×12×5×cos⁡90∘\text{or, } V = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2 + 2 \times 12 \times 5 \times \cos 90^\circ}
∴V=13 ms−1\therefore V = 13\text{ ms}^{-1} Velocity of the rain relative to the boatman,
urb=vr2+V2−2vrVcos⁡βu_{rb} = \sqrt{v_r^2 + V^2 - 2v_r V \cos\beta}
or, vrb=82+132−2×8×13×cos⁡60∘\text{or, } v_{rb} = \sqrt{8^2 + 13^2 - 2 \times 8 \times 13 \times \cos 60^\circ}
or, vrb=129\text{or, } v_{rb} = \sqrt{129}
or, vrb=11.358 ms−1 Ans.\text{or, } v_{rb} = 11.358\text{ ms}^{-1}\text{ Ans.}

[book printing error: here '+ 2v_r V cosβ' is written and in the next line '- 2 × 8 × 13 × cos60°'; according to the subtraction of relative velocity the '-' sign itself is correct]

Part d · 4 marks

Verify whether the boatman will be saved from the rain if he holds the umbrella at an angle 45∘45^\circ with the vertical.

Hint Try it first

Compare the ratio of apparent horizontal to downward rain speed with tan 45°.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Obtained from (c), the resultant velocity of the boat and the current, V=13 ms−1V = 13\text{ ms}^{-1}. This resultant velocity acts parallel to the river or in the horizontal plane. According to the stem, the rain is falling at an angle 60∘60^\circ with this resultant velocity. Let the horizontal component of the velocity of the rain be vrxv_{rx} and the vertical component vryv_{ry}.
vrx=vrcos⁡60∘v_{rx} = v_r\cos 60^\circ

vrx=8×0.5v_{rx} = 8 \times 0.5

vrx=4 ms−1v_{rx} = 4\text{ ms}^{-1}
vry=vrsin⁡60∘v_{ry} = v_r\sin 60^\circ

vry=8×sin⁡60∘v_{ry} = 8 \times \sin 60^\circ

vry=6.928 ms−1v_{ry} = 6.928\text{ ms}^{-1} The components of the velocity of the rain relative to the boatman are: Horizontal component, Vx=vrx−V=4−13=−9ms−1V_x = v_{rx} - V = 4 - 13 = -9\text{ms}^{-1} Vertical component, Vy=vry=6.928 ms−1V_y = v_{ry} = 6.928\text{ ms}^{-1} Let the relative velocity of the rain make an angle θ\theta with the vertical. We know,
tan⁡θ=∣Vx∣∣Vy∣\tan\theta = \frac{|V_x|}{|V_y|}

tan⁡θ=96.928\tan\theta = \frac{9}{6.928}

tan⁡θ=1.299\tan\theta = 1.299
or, θ=tan⁡−1(1.299)\text{or, } \theta = \tan^{-1}(1.299)

or, θ=52.41∘\text{or, } \theta = 52.41^\circ It is seen that to be saved from the rain the boatman must hold the umbrella at an angle 52.41∘52.41^\circ with the vertical. But the boatman held the umbrella at an angle 45∘45^\circ with the vertical. Since 52.41∘≠45∘52.41^\circ \neq 45^\circ, therefore it can be said that the boatman will not be saved from the rain if he holds the umbrella at an angle 45∘45^\circ with the vertical.
