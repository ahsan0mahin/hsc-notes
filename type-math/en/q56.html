River–boat and current

Question 56

Adamjee Cantonment College, Dhaka · Board question no. 1 · book p. 58

Class begins in 25 min25\text{ min}, so OO has to be reached from BB. Three students set off after requesting three boatmen. With the 1st student the boatman sets off along OBOB and reaches CC. After that the student walks with velocity 1.6 ms−11.6\text{ ms}^{-1} and reaches point BB. With the 2nd student the boatman sets off along OBOB and reaches CC. Then, rowing the boat again, he comes from CC to point BB . With the 3rd student the boatman sets off along OAOA and reaches point BB directly. The velocities of the boat with the current and against the current are 15 kmh−115\text{ kmh}^{-1} and 5 kmh−15\text{ kmh}^{-1}.

[printed as 'is' in the book]

Part c · 3 marks

Determine the distance of point CC from BB.

Hint Try it first

Infer both still-water and current speeds from the upstream and downstream values.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Adding equations (i) and (ii) we get,
(u+v)+(u−v)=15+5(u + v) + (u - v) = 15 + 5

(u+v)+(u−v)=20(u + v) + (u - v) = 20

(u+v)+(u−v)=10 kmh−1(u + v) + (u - v) = 10\text{ kmh}^{-1} Putting the value of uu from equation (i) we get,
10+v=1510 + v = 15

10+v=5 kmh−110 + v = 5\text{ kmh}^{-1}
or, Let, the velocity of the boat uu; the velocity of the current vv Here, Velocity of the boat with the current, u+v=15 kmh−1u + v = 15\text{ kmh}^{-1} (i) Velocity of the boat against the current, u−v=5 kmh−1u - v = 5\text{ kmh}^{-1} (ii) Width of the river, d=OB=2 kmd = OB = 2\text{ km}
In the case of the 1st student, the boatman sets off along OBOB and reaches point CC. (That is, the boat sets off perpendicular to the current.) So, the time required for the 1st student to cross the river,
tOC=width of the rivervelocity of the boatt_{OC} = \frac{\text{width of the river}}{\text{velocity of the boat}}

tOC=dut_{OC} = \frac{d}{u}

tOC=210 ht_{OC} = \frac{2}{10}\text{ h}

tOC=0.2 ht_{OC} = 0.2\text{ h} The distance the boat covers due to the current during this time is the distance BCBC.
BC=velocity of the current×timeBC = \text{velocity of the current} \times \text{time}

BC=v×tOCBC = v \times t_{OC}

BC=5×0.2 kmBC = 5 \times 0.2\text{ km}

BC=1 km Ans.BC = 1\text{ km Ans.}

Part d · 4 marks

Will the three students be able to reach class on time? Discuss mathematically.

Hint Try it first

Separate the crossing time from each student’s extra walking or rowing time.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

In the case of the 1st student (by boat O→CO \to C, on foot C→BC \to B): time to go from OO to CC, tOC=0.2 ht_{OC} = 0.2\text{ h} (obtained in part c) Time to walk from CC to BB,
tCB, walking=BCvwt_{CB,\,\text{walking}} = \frac{BC}{v_w}

tCB, walking=1 km5.76 kmh−1t_{CB,\,\text{walking}} = \frac{1\text{ km}}{5.76\text{ kmh}^{-1}}

tCB, walking=0.1736 ht_{CB,\,\text{walking}} = 0.1736\text{ h}
total time, t1=tOC+tCB, walkingt_1 = t_{OC} + t_{CB,\,\text{walking}}
or, t1=0.2+0.1736 h\text{or, } t_1 = 0.2 + 0.1736\text{ h}

or, t1=0.3736 h\text{or, } t_1 = 0.3736\text{ h}

or, t1=0.3736×60 min\text{or, } t_1 = 0.3736 \times 60\text{ min}

or, t1=22.42 min\text{or, } t_1 = 22.42\text{ min} Since t1<25 mint_1 < 25\text{ min}, therefore the 1st student will be able to arrive on time.

In the case of the 2nd student (by boat O→CO \to C, by boat C→BC \to B): time to go from OO to CC, tOC=0.2 ht_{OC} = 0.2\text{ h} (obtained in part c) time to go by boat from CC to BB upstream against the current and from CC to BB,
tCB, boat=BCu−vt_{CB,\,\text{boat}} = \frac{BC}{u - v}

tCB, boat=1 km5 kmh−1t_{CB,\,\text{boat}} = \frac{1\text{ km}}{5\text{ kmh}^{-1}}

tCB, boat=0.2 ht_{CB,\,\text{boat}} = 0.2\text{ h}

(upper box) distance BCBC, BC=1 kmBC = 1\text{ km} (obtained from part c) Velocity of the boat against the current, u−v=5 kmh−1u - v = 5\text{ kmh}^{-1}
total time, t2=tOC+tCB, boatt_2 = t_{OC} + t_{CB,\,\text{boat}}
or, t2=0.2+0.2 h\text{or, } t_2 = 0.2 + 0.2\text{ h}

or, t2=0.4 h\text{or, } t_2 = 0.4\text{ h}

or, t2=0.4×60 min\text{or, } t_2 = 0.4 \times 60\text{ min}

or, t2=24 min\text{or, } t_2 = 24\text{ min} Since t2<25 mint_2 < 25\text{ min}, therefore the 2nd student will be able to arrive on time.

In the case of the 3rd student (O→BO \to B directly): For the 3rd student to reach point BB directly, the boat must travel against the current (along OAOA). In this case the resultant velocity RR will act along the width of the river. Similarly,
R=u2−v2R = \sqrt{u^2 - v^2}

R=102−52R = \sqrt{10^2 - 5^2}

R=100−25R = \sqrt{100 - 25}

R=75R = \sqrt{75}

R=8.660 kmh−1R = 8.660\text{ kmh}^{-1} required time to cross the river,
t3=dRt_3 = \frac{d}{R}

t3=28.660 ht_3 = \frac{2}{8.660}\text{ h}

t3=0.2309 ht_3 = 0.2309\text{ h}

t3=0.2309×60 mint_3 = 0.2309 \times 60\text{ min}

t3=13.85 mint_3 = 13.85\text{ min} Since t3<25 mint_3 < 25\text{ min}, therefore the 3rd student will be able to arrive on time. It is seen that the time for the 1st student is 22.42 min22.42\text{ min}, for the 2nd student 24 min24\text{ min} and for the 3rd student 13.85 min13.85\text{ min}. Since in all three cases the required time is less than 25 min25\text{ min}, therefore the three students will be able to reach class on time.
