Question 54

Holy Cross College, Dhaka · Board question no. 1 · book p. 57

Scenario-1: In the laboratory, a slide calliper was used to measure the diameter of a solid cylinder. The main-scale reading was 4.2 cm4.2\text{ cm} and the vernier coincidence was 88. The vernier constant of the instrument is 0.01cm0.01\text{cm}. The cylinder is rolling down an inclined plane.

Scenario-2: While the cylinder is rotating, its angular velocity vector is ω⃗=2i^−3j^+k^ rad/s\vec{\omega} = 2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}\text{ rad/s} and its radius vector is r⃗=i^−2j^+2k^ m\vec{r} = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}\text{ m}.

Part c · 3 marks

Determine the diameter of the cylinder in Scenario-1 and find the relative error in determining the volume (assume the height is error-free 10 cm10\text{ cm}).

Hint Try it first

Combine the two instrument readings. With exact height, how does volume depend on diameter?

Full solution Diagram and explanation

We know,
diameter, D=M+V×VC\text{diameter, } D = M + V \times VC

diameter, D=4.2+8×0.01\text{diameter, } D = 4.2 + 8 \times 0.01

diameter, D=4.2+0.08\text{diameter, } D = 4.2 + 0.08

diameter, D=4.28 cm\text{diameter, } D = 4.28\text{ cm}
In the case of a slide calliper the final error in measurement is equal to the vernier constant. Final error in the diameter measurement, ΔD=VC=0.01 cm\Delta D = VC = 0.01\text{ cm} Diameter of the cylinder, D=4.28 cmD = 4.28\text{ cm}, height of the cylinder, h=10 cmh = 10\text{ cm} Actual volume of the cylinder,
V=π4D2hV = \frac{\pi}{4} D^2 h

V=π4×(4.28)2×10V = \frac{\pi}{4} \times (4.28)^2 \times 10

V=143.87 cm3V = 143.87\text{ cm}^3 Maximum value in the diameter measurement, Dmax⁡=D+ΔD=4.28+0.01=4.29 cmD_{\max} = D + \Delta D = 4.28 + 0.01 = 4.29\text{ cm} Maximum volume of the cylinder,
Vmax⁡=π4Dmax⁡2hV_{\max} = \frac{\pi}{4} D_{\max}^2 h

Vmax⁡=π4×(4.29)2×10V_{\max} = \frac{\pi}{4} \times (4.29)^2 \times 10

Vmax⁡=144.54 cm3V_{\max} = 144.54\text{ cm}^3 Minimum value in the diameter measurement, Dmin⁡=D−ΔD=4.28−0.01=4.27 cmD_{\min} = D - \Delta D = 4.28 - 0.01 = 4.27\text{ cm} Minimum volume of the cylinder,
Vmin⁡=π4Dmin⁡2hV_{\min} = \frac{\pi}{4} D_{\min}^2 h

Vmin⁡=π4×(4.27)2×10V_{\min} = \frac{\pi}{4} \times (4.27)^2 \times 10

Vmin⁡=143.2 cm3V_{\min} = 143.2\text{ cm}^3 Final error for the maximum volume, ΔV1=∣Vmax⁡−V∣=∣144.54−143.87∣=0.67 cm3\Delta V_1 = |V_{\max} - V| = |144.54 - 143.87| = 0.67\text{ cm}^3 Final error for the minimum volume, ΔV2=∣Vmin⁡−V∣=∣143.2−143.87∣=0.67 cm3\Delta V_2 = |V_{\min} - V| = |143.2 - 143.87| = 0.67\text{ cm}^3 Final error in measuring the volume of the cube [cylinder], ΔV=0.67 cm3\Delta V = 0.67\text{ cm}^3 Relative error in determining the volume of the cube [cylinder], ΔVV=0.67143.87=0.00465\frac{\Delta V}{V} = \frac{0.67}{143.87} = 0.00465 Percentage relative error = 0.00465×100%=0.465%0.00465 \times 100\% = 0.465\% Ans.

Part d · 4 marks

In the light of the information in Scenario-2, determine the linear velocity vector of the cylinder and show that the linear velocity is perpendicular to the radius vector.

Hint Try it first

Calculate ω×r in that order, then dot the result with r.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

We know, Linear velocity vector,
v⃗=ω⃗×r⃗\vec{v} = \vec{\omega} \times \vec{r}

v⃗=(2i^−3j^+k^)×(i^−2j^+2k^)\vec{v} = (2\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} + \hat{k}) \times (\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k})
∣i^j^k^2−311−22∣=(−6+2)i^−(4−1)j^+(−4+3)k^\left|\begin{matrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & -3 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 & 2 \end{matrix}\right| = (-6 + 2)\hat{i} - (4 - 1)\hat{j} + (-4 + 3)\hat{k}
∴v⃗=−4i^−3j^−k^ ms−1\therefore \vec{v} = -4\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} - \hat{k}\text{ ms}^{-1}
Again, two vectors will be mutually perpendicular if their dot product is zero. Here, for v⃗=−4i^−3j^−k^\vec{v} = -4\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} - \hat{k} and r⃗=i^−2j^+2k^\vec{r} = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}, now, v⃗⋅r⃗=(−4i^−3j^−k^)⋅(i^−2j^+2k^)=−4+6−2=0\vec{v} \cdot \vec{r} = (-4\hat{i} - 3\hat{j} - \hat{k}) \cdot (\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}) = -4 + 6 - 2 = 0 Since the dot product of the linear velocity vector v⃗\vec{v} and the radius vector r⃗\vec{r} is zero, therefore the linear velocity vector is perpendicular to the radius vector.
