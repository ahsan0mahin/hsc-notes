Rain and umbrella

Question 28

Dhaka Board · 2023 · Board question no. 8 · book p. 44

The rear glass of a car is inclined at an angle 30∘30^\circ with the roof. The car was moving along a road with velocity v⃗=18i^\vec{v} = 18\hat{i}. Suddenly rain began to fall with velocity u⃗=−12j^\vec{u} = -12\hat{j}.

Part c · 3 marks

At what velocity will the rain strike the front glass of the car?

Hint Try it first

Work in the car frame: subtract the entire car velocity vector from the rain velocity.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

v⃗rc=u⃗−v⃗=−18i^−12j^\vec v_{rc}=\vec u-\vec v=-18\hat i-12\hat j

Subtract the car velocity: relative velocity is (−18,−12), of magnitude 6√13≈21.63 in the given speed units.

Part d · 4 marks

Will the rear glass of the car in the stem get wet in the rain — give your opinion with mathematical analysis.

Hint Try it first

Compare the downward slope of the relative rain path with the slope of the rear window.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Magnitude of the velocity of the rain falling vertically,

u=∣u⃗∣u=|\vec u|

u=12 unitu = 12\ \text{unit}
and the magnitude of the velocity of the car,

v=∣v⃗∣v=|\vec v|

v=18 unit.v = 18\ \text{unit}.
If the resultant velocity of the rain falls at an angle less than 60° with the vertical, the rear glass of the car will get wet. Otherwise it will not.
If the direction of the resultant velocity of the rain is θ\theta, then

tan⁡θ=vsin⁡90∘u+vcos⁡90∘\tan\theta=\frac{v\sin90^\circ}{u+v\cos90^\circ}

tan⁡θ=v×1u+v×0\tan\theta = \frac{v\times1}{u+v\times0}

tan⁡θ=vu\tan\theta = \frac vu
or,

θ=tan⁡−1(vu)\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac vu\right)

θ=tan⁡−1(1812)\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{18}{12}\right)

θ=56.31∘<60∘.\theta = 56.31^\circ<60^\circ.
Since the direction of the resultant velocity of the rain is less than 60°, therefore the rear glass of the car will get wet in the rain.

No unit of velocity is given in the stem, so the original answer also says 'at unit velocity'.
