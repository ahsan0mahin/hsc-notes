Part c · 3 marks

Hint Try it first

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Part d · null marks

Do the two vectors obey the commutative law of vector multiplication? Analyse mathematically.

Hint Try it first

Compare the two products by reversing the order, and rule out a zero cross product.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Using the value of mm obtained from part (c) we get, First vector, A⃗=2i^+3j^−5k^\vec{A} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k} Second vector, B⃗=−28i^+2j^−10k^\vec{B} = -28\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 10\hat{k} According to the commutative law of vector multiplication, A⃗×B⃗=B⃗×A⃗\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = \vec{B} \times \vec{A} [assumed for verification]
A⃗×B⃗=∣i^j^k^23−5−282−10∣\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = \left|\begin{matrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & 3 & -5 \\ -28 & 2 & -10 \end{matrix}\right|
or, A⃗×B⃗=i^{3(−10)−(−5)2}−j^{2(−10)−(−5)(−28)}+k^{2(2)−3(−28)}\text{or, } \vec{A} \times \vec{B} = \hat{i}\{3(-10) - (-5)2\} - \hat{j}\{2(-10) - (-5)(-28)\} + \hat{k}\{2(2) - 3(-28)\}
or, A⃗×B⃗=i^(−30+10)−j^(−20−140)+k^(4+84)\text{or, } \vec{A} \times \vec{B} = \hat{i}(-30 + 10) - \hat{j}(-20 - 140) + \hat{k}(4 + 84)
∴A⃗×B⃗=−20i^+160j^+88k^\therefore \vec{A} \times \vec{B} = -20\hat{i} + 160\hat{j} + 88\hat{k}
B⃗×A⃗=∣i^j^k^−282−1023−5∣\vec{B} \times \vec{A} = \left|\begin{matrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ -28 & 2 & -10 \\ 2 & 3 & -5 \end{matrix}\right|
i^{2(−5)−(−10)3}−j^{(−28)(−5)−(−10)2}+k^{(−28)3−2(2)}\hat{i}\{2(-5) - (-10)3\} - \hat{j}\{(-28)(-5) - (-10)2\} + \hat{k}\{(-28)3 - 2(2)\}
i^(−10+30)−j^(140+20)+k^(−84−4)\hat{i}(-10 + 30) - \hat{j}(140 + 20) + \hat{k}(-84 - 4)
∴B⃗×A⃗=20i^−160j^−88k^\therefore \vec{B} \times \vec{A} = 20\hat{i} - 160\hat{j} - 88\hat{k}
B⃗×A⃗=−(−20i^+160j^+88k^)\vec{B} \times \vec{A} = -(-20\hat{i} + 160\hat{j} + 88\hat{k})

B⃗×A⃗=−(A⃗×B⃗)\vec{B} \times \vec{A} = -(\vec{A} \times \vec{B}) it is seen that A⃗×B⃗≠B⃗×A⃗\vec{A} \times \vec{B} \neq \vec{B} \times \vec{A}. Therefore it can be said that the two vectors do not obey the commutative law of vector multiplication.
