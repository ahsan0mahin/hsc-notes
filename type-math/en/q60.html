Rain and umbrella

Question 60

BCIC College, Dhaka · Board question no. 1 · book p. 61

Three points 4.62 ms−14.62\text{ ms}^{-1}, vv and uu lie in a three-dimensional rectangular coordinate system.

Part c · 3 marks

While moving, the front glass of the car got wet but the rear glass did not — explain with a drawing.

Hint Try it first

Draw three separate arrows for the falling rain, the wind and the car’s motion.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

Velocity of the wind relative to the car,
vwc⃗=vw⃗−vc⃗\vec{v_{wc}} = \vec{v_w} - \vec{v_c}

vwc⃗=vw⃗+(−vc⃗) (backwards)\vec{v_{wc}} = \vec{v_w} + (-\vec{v_c})\text{ (backwards)} Velocity of the rain relative to the car,
vrc⃗=vr⃗−(vwc⃗)\vec{v_{rc}} = \vec{v_r} - (\vec{v_{wc}})

vrc⃗=vr⃗−(vw⃗−vc⃗)\vec{v_{rc}} = \vec{v_r} - (\vec{v_w} - \vec{v_c})

(writing on the right side) then, Actual velocity of the rain, vr⃗\vec{v_r} (vertically downwards) Velocity of the car, vc⃗\vec{v_c} (horizontally forwards) Velocity of the wind, vw⃗\vec{v_w} (horizontally backwards) Velocity of the rain relative to the car, vrc⃗\vec{v_{rc}}

Part d · 4 marks

Will a person sitting on the roof of a car be saved from the rain if he holds the umbrella at an angle 40∘40^\circ? Explain through mathematical analysis.

Hint Try it first

Check which wind speed is actually given. The direction alone does not determine the apparent angle.

Full solution Diagram and explanation

The person sitting on the roof of the car is moving with the same velocity as the car. So, to be saved from the rain, he must hold the umbrella in the direction of the velocity of the rain relative to the car.
Resultant velocity of the rain (relative to the ground),

V⃗net R=v⃗r+v⃗w\vec V_{\mathrm{net}\,R}=\vec v_r+\vec v_w

V⃗net R=(−2i^−8j^) m s−1.\vec V_{\mathrm{net}\,R}=(-2\hat i-8\hat j)\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
Velocity of the rain relative to the car,

v⃗rc=V⃗net R−v⃗C\vec v_{rc}=\vec V_{\mathrm{net}\,R}-\vec v_C

v⃗rc=(−2i^−8j^)−(4.62i^)\vec v_{rc}=(-2\hat i-8\hat j)-(4.62\hat i)

v⃗rc=(−2−4.62)i^−8j^\vec v_{rc}=(-2-4.62)\hat i-8\hat j

v⃗rc=−6.62i^−8j^ m s−1.\vec v_{rc}=-6.62\hat i-8\hat j\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
Here, the velocity of the car vC=4.62 m s−1v_C=4.62\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} (towards v); the actual velocity of the rain vr=8 m s−1v_r=8\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} (towards M); the velocity of the wind vw=2 m s−1v_w=2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} (towards u).
If the relative velocity of the rain makes an angle θ\theta with the vertical:

tan⁡θ=horizontal componentvertical component\tan\theta=\frac{\text{horizontal component}}{\text{vertical component}}

tan⁡θ=∣vrc,x∣∣vrc,y∣\tan\theta = \frac{|v_{rc,x}|}{|v_{rc,y}|}
or, tan⁡θ=6.62/8\tan\theta=6.62/8 or, tan⁡θ=0.8275\tan\theta=0.8275 or, θ=tan⁡−1(0.8275)\theta=\tan^{-1}(0.8275)

∴θ=39.62∘.\therefore\theta=39.62^\circ.
Therefore, to be saved from the rain, the person sitting on the roof of the car must hold the umbrella tilted forwards at an angle of 39.62° with the vertical.
Since the man held the umbrella at an angle of 40° and 39.62∘≈40∘39.62^\circ\approx40^\circ, therefore it can be said that the man will be saved from the rain.
Although the direction of the wind is given in the stem on the previous page, the 2 m s⁻¹ value of its velocity is not found there; that value has been taken in this answer. So the result 39.62° depends on this additional value. The correct addition in this part v⃗r+v⃗w−v⃗C\vec v_r+\vec v_w-\vec v_C corrects the sign inconsistency in the earlier (c). Without knowing the size of the umbrella and the spread of the rain, certain protection from a nearby angle cannot be proved; the book's conclusion is approximate.
